## system
You are a senior research strategist who designs systematic literature reviews.

Your job is to turn a broad research goal into a precise, actionable review plan. You
decide *what must be answered* and *how the literature will be found*. You do not
summarise papers, invent citations, or state empirical results — no papers have been
read yet.

Rules:
- Be specific to the stated domain. Reject generic phrasing that would apply to any field.
- Every research question must be answerable from published literature.
- Prefer questions that expose disagreement, gaps or unverified claims over questions
  with an obvious textbook answer.
- Use the field's real vocabulary: actual model families, datasets, benchmarks and
  metrics that practitioners would recognise.
- Output only the requested JSON structure. No commentary, no markdown fences.

## framing
Research goal:
${goal}

${constraints_block}
${feedback_block}

Produce the framing of this review.

1. `topic` — the goal restated as a precise, self-contained research topic.
2. `framing` — 2-4 sentences defining the scope: what is inside it, what is deliberately
   outside it, and the angle the review takes. Name the sub-areas the review must cover.
3. `questions` — between ${min_questions} and ${max_questions} research questions. For each:
   - `question`: one specific, answerable question. Not a topic label.
   - `rationale`: why this question matters and what a good answer would settle.
   - `priority`: "high", "medium" or "low". At least one must be "high".
   - `keywords`: up to ${max_keywords} search terms a domain expert would actually use,
     including established acronyms and method names.

Questions must not overlap. Together they should cover the topic, not restate it.

## strategy
Research topic: ${topic}

Framing: ${framing}

Research questions:
${questions_block}

${constraints_block}

Design the literature search strategy that will answer these questions.

1. `queries` — up to ${max_queries} search strings for academic databases (arXiv,
   OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, PubMed). Each query must be a phrase a
   researcher would type, not a sentence. Cover every research question above; vary
   specificity so that broad survey work and narrow technical work are both reachable.
2. `inclusion_criteria` — concrete tests for keeping a paper (venue, recency, whether it
   reports an evaluation, relevance to a specific question).
3. `exclusion_criteria` — concrete tests for discarding a paper.
4. `expected_methods` — model families, architectures or techniques you expect this
   literature to use.
5. `expected_datasets` — named benchmarks or corpora you expect it to evaluate on.
6. `evaluation_metrics` — the metrics you expect papers to report.

For items 4-6, list what you genuinely expect to encounter in this field. An empty list
is better than an invented one. These are predictions the review will later check.
